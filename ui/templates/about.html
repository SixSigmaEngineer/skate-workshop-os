A workshop operating system

Capture the conversation. Connect the evidence. Land the next move.

SKATE stands for Scalable Knowledge Architecture & Technology Engine. It brings meeting capture, workshop coaching, connected knowledge, and follow-through into one local workspace. A recording starts the story. SKATE helps carry it into the next decision, the next workshop, and the work that follows.

Hybrid knowledge graph + vector search

SKATE combines explicit relationships, semantic meaning, and exact terms. Each gives you a different way to find and understand the evidence.

Knowledge graph
Preserves named links between notes: supports, contradicts, causes, and leads to, alongside references, similarity links, and shared themes. The GRIND makes those connections visible in 2D and 3D.
Vector memory
Stores note embeddings locally and retrieves by meaning when an embedding backend is available. A question about “pricing” can find evidence written as “cost,” even when the wording differs.
Keyword precision
Keeps names, project terms, and exact wording in the search. It also keeps retrieval working when embeddings are unavailable.

Your notes remain readable, portable markdown. Vector search finds related meaning; the graph preserves stated relationships you can inspect. “Similar to” and “contradicts” tell you different things about a finding.

Inside the hybrid memory

SKATE keeps a rebuildable, content-hashed vector cache on disk and calculates cosine similarity over those embeddings. This supplies the vector-search layer without a separate vector database server. When embeddings are available, retrieval blends 60% semantic similarity with 40% normalized keyword scoring.

Local Ollama with nomic-embed-text and local fastembed can be detected automatically. OpenAI embeddings require an explicitly selected backend and credentials. Without an embedding backend, retrieval uses keywords. Agents can retrieve bounded sets of relevant note summaries and trace explicit graph links back to evidence.

Small context. Sources intact.

Give your agent the evidence that fits the question. SKATE’s MCP connection returns a bounded set of note summaries with source references. You can open a source for more detail or trace its relationships. The notes remain readable files in your vault, ready for the next workshop or a different agent.

Keep the trail behind the recommendation. A pain can link to the observations supporting it, a conflicting finding, and the action it led to. Six weeks later, the team can inspect why it made that decision. Semantic similarity helps find related notes; named graph relationships record how those notes connect.

MCP agents can add notes and sessions with provenance. They have no tools to overwrite or delete existing notes. Session scope and active status control which evidence appears in governed retrieval. You retain the original records and review the work in SKATE.

Human factors built into the workflow

SKATE is designed around the person doing the thinking. Cognitive psychology and human factors inform how you capture, review, and recall evidence, so you can keep your attention on the room and the decisions ahead.

Chunk the signal

Working memory is limited. Short markers such as #O for observation, #P for pain, #Q for question, and #A for action group a conversation into meaningful units. The design draws on Cowan’s work on working-memory capacity.

Think at capture

Marking a finding as a pain or decision asks you to consider its meaning while the context is fresh. This reflects the levels-of-processing framework and preserves your interpretation alongside the evidence.

Reduce the search burden

Clear labels, visible relationships, and reviewed cleanup help you find the material that matters. The application of cognitive-load theory is practical: spend less attention sorting material and more understanding it.

Give people the judgment

You decide what matters and review what gets kept. SKATE stores, searches, and connects it. The GRIND proposes outputs with evidence to check; The Lineup gives the resulting actions owners, due dates, and a place in the next day’s work.

The neuroscience connection is deliberate resource allocation. A 2026 Georgia Tech–Cornell study examined how different brain wiring systems expand and contract across species under resource constraints. SKATE takes a design analogy from that work: allocate limited attention and model context to the evidence the task needs. The study investigated brain architecture; SKATE’s benefits require their own measurements.

AI context needs care, too. Lost in the Middle and NoLiMa found weaknesses in the long-context tasks they tested. SKATE addresses that practical problem with bounded evidence retrieval and cleanup you review before applying.

Where SKATE earns its edge

Free and open source under the MIT license. Local recording, transcription, and the core workshop workflow require no SKATE subscription. Optional cloud AI services use your provider account and may charge for usage. See how SKATE compares with Granola.

The advantage is the complete workshop loop. Record the conversation, identify the signal, connect supporting and conflicting evidence, and carry a recommendation into an action with its source intact.

  • Defensible recommendations: follow relationships from an action or decision through the pains and observations behind it.
  • Continuity between workshops: organize notes by session and engagement. Retire inactive sessions from scoped GRIND and agent retrieval while keeping their records.
  • Control over your knowledge: local recording and transcription, portable markdown, selectable AI providers, and a core workflow that works without AI.
  • Visible follow-through: promote an action into The Lineup, assign an owner and due date, mark it Landed, and use recurring Standard Work for the habits that need to stick.
Measured retrieval performance

A relevant note ranked first for 9 of 10 queries in SKATE’s internal demo-vault benchmark, rerun in September 2026. That was about 4.2× the expected chance baseline for those queries.

The test used 25 active notes across two sessions, 10 author-labelled queries, and keyword-only retrieval. Mean reciprocal rank was 0.95; the exact 95% confidence interval for the top-result hit rate was 55.5–99.7%. Five-result payloads averaged about 2,200 tokens, estimated from character count.

This is a small internal retrieval check. It does not establish superiority over competitors or measure cognitive benefits for users. Those claims need controlled comparisons of accuracy, workload, and adoption. The reproducible benchmark is included at tools/benchmark_retrieval.py.

What you can do today

  • Record a Meeting: capture PC audio and your microphone locally from Spotter Live, then save both WAV audio and a linked transcript note. Create a session in the recording dropdown. Closing the desktop window hides it while recording continues. The tray switches from a skateboard to a red circle during recording; hover for status or right-click to stop and save.
  • Bring your recordings: import audio or video, with configurable uploads up to 4 GB and local transcription in chunks.
  • Clean with control: review and edit proposed note cleanup before applying it, see excluded chatter, and keep the original transcript attached.
  • Work with Spotter: get coaching grounded in the session and microphone captions using a selected local or cloud transcription option.
  • Run The GRIND: explore the graph in 2D or 3D and synthesize session evidence into pain points, How-Might-We prompts, and solution starters. Export synthesis to Excel, save notes, and print a note or session to PDF.
  • Connect your agents: the MCP connection provides bounded memory retrieval, evidence tracing, and additive notes and sessions with agent provenance.

The core note, graph, keyword-search, and local synthesis workflow works without AI. PC recording and upload transcription stay local. When you choose cloud AI or cloud live captions, the relevant text or audio goes to that provider.
